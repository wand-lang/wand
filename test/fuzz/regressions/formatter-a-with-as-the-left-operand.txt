A `with` body goes as far right as it can, so a `with` that is the left
operand of an operator needs its bracket. `emit_binop` kept the bracket on
`try`, `fn`, `match`, `let` and the other forms of this kind, but not on
`with`. So the first pass changed the program:

    (with a as d -> (x; y)) - 1
    with a as d -> (x; y) - 1     <- the `- 1` is now in the body

When the body wrapped, the first pass put the body's `(` on the `->`
line. The second pass read the `(` as the left operand of `-`, which is
not a bracket that a `with` body opens on its own, and moved it to a new
line. That difference is what the fuzzer saw. `emit_chain` already kept
the bracket on `with`; `emit_binop` now does too.
