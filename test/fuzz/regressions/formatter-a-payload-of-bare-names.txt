A constructor takes the bracket written after it, and the parser reads
bare names in that bracket as the names a construction puns. `T(m)` and
`T(a, b)` are `ConstrBare`, which a record's constructor takes as its
fields. `T(m;)` and `T((a, b);)` are the constructor applied to the value,
the same as `T m` and `T ((a, b))`.

The formatter wrote the application with the payload in that bracket, so
it came back as the pun:

  f T(m;)       -->  f T (m)      -->  f T(m)
  T((a, b);)    -->  T (a, b)     -->  T(a, b)

Qualified, it settled, but on the other program: `l.T(m;)` came back as
`l.T(m)`.

A payload of bare names takes the whole bracket in an argument,
`f (T m)`, a tuple of them a second bracket, `T ((a, b))`, and qualified,
`l.T((m))` and `l.T((a, b))`.
